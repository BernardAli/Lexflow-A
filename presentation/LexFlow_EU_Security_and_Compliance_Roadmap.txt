01 / DECISION BRIEF
A credible path to high assurance
The project can be developed toward demanding EU requirements. The current pilot is a
foundation, not evidence that every obligation has been satisfied.
LexFlow supports intake, extraction, source review, approval and controlled export for a dedicated German
notary office. Its strongest existing safeguards are human review gates, encrypted office data, frozen approval
snapshots and verifiable handoff receipts. The intended product remains an assistant to accountable
professionals.
What “highest” should mean for this project
Use a demanding, risk-based security target: applicable law, documented safeguards, independent challenge
and sustained operating evidence. There is no single universal EU certificate that proves every software
product meets the highest possible safety and security level.
Decision
Required outcome
Keep the purpose narrow
Propose case data with evidence; require professional review. Do not introduce
autonomous legal conclusions or irreversible filing without a fresh assessment.
Harden before real data
Approve supplier and privacy arrangements; enforce strong identity controls; validate
the actual hosting, monitoring, key custody and recovery environment.
Prove each handoff
Keep ProNotar32 unavailable until the vendor contract, adapter, authorization and
durable import acceptance are demonstrated.
Make assurance repeatable
Maintain control owners, test reports, incident drills, access reviews and independent
assessment. Reassess material product and supplier changes.
Reading guide
Pages 3–4: applicability and dates. Pages 5–6: current baseline and target architecture. Pages 7–13: privacy,
security, AI safety, integration and operations. Pages 14–16: governance, delivery and release gates. Pages
17–19: evidence and official sources.
Status vocabulary: IMPLEMENTED PILOT means supported by reviewed code or recorded local verification. REQUIRED means
proposed work or evidence for the selected assurance target. CONDITIONAL means applicability depends on legal classification.
UNVERIFIED means the relevant environment or outcome has not been demonstrated. A legal adviser should confirm the
applicability record before production use.

06 / DATA PROTECTION AND SECRECY
Make privacy operational
GDPR accountability needs records, decisions and working procedures alongside encryption.
1. Establish purposes and responsibilities
The notary will ordinarily control the purposes of office case processing; a hosted LexFlow operator may be a
processor. Confirm the actual arrangement, including any separate operator purposes. Document lawful bases
by workflow, extra conditions for sensitive data, recipients and notices. Do not default to consent as the basis
for every notarial activity. Maintain processing records and assess DPO requirements. [R01]
2. Assess effects before using real files
Complete and record a DPIA screening. Given confidential documents and potentially sensitive personal
information, the proposed assurance target is a full DPIA before the live pilot. Describe necessity, alternatives,
misuse, inaccurate extraction, unauthorized access and mitigation. Seek required prior consultation if high
residual risk cannot be reduced. [R01]
3. Control suppliers and international access
Agree processor terms, approved subprocessors, confidentiality, incident assistance, deletion/return, audit rights
and exit arrangements. The preferred deployment profile keeps processing, backups and support within
controlled EU/EEA arrangements. Hosting region alone does not resolve third-country access. Where transfers
occur, establish a valid transfer mechanism and assess safeguards and foreign-access risk. EU-only hosting is a
project choice, not a universal GDPR rule. [R03]
4. Respect notarial professional secrecy
BNotO §26a requires careful service-provider selection and contractual secrecy safeguards. Its paragraph 4
requires participant consent for access to secrets when a service directly serves an individual official matter.
Have notarial counsel determine whether the proposed AI or support service falls within that provision. This is a
separate question from the GDPR lawful basis. [R13]
Acceptance evidence
Approved data-flow inventory, processing records, DPIA and risk decisions; notices and supplier agreements;
professional-secrecy assessment; transfer assessment where needed; a demonstrated
access/correction/deletion request procedure with documented legal exceptions. The software must not
silently promise erasure of legally retained records.
Sources: [R01] EDPB accountability · [R03] EDPB transfers · [R13] BNotO §26a

08 / AI SAFETY AND HUMAN OVERSIGHT
Keep human judgment in control
The main safety risks are false confidence, missed conflicts, mixed identities and unreviewed
downstream action.
Classify the intended use. Annex III includes certain systems assisting judicial authorities with interpreting
facts/law or applying law. Administrative extraction for a notary is not automatically that use. If an Annex III
category applies, a narrow preparatory-task exception requires a reasoned assessment; human review alone is
not a blanket exemption. Profiling within the relevant Annex III context cannot use that exception. Document
and register an Article 6(3) exemption where required. [R06, R08]
Hazard
Required safeguard and evaluation
Invented or misplaced facts
Retain source provenance and originals. Check that cited evidence supports the field
and belongs to the correct person/property. An exact quote match alone does not
establish semantic correctness.
Automation bias
Show missing values, competing evidence and unread pages. Require active review of
critical fields and original scans. Allow correction, abstention and a fully manual route;
never label heuristic scores as validated accuracy.
Prompt injection
Treat document content as data, including embedded instructions. Restrict model
output to validated schemas. No model-controlled tools, destinations, policy changes
or direct approval. Exercise adversarial documents.
Model or workflow drift
Version model/provider settings, prompts, normalizers and mappings. Re-evaluate
before changes. Include German names, multiple parties, WEG, poor scans, dates,
amounts, conflicting drafts and omitted pages.
Uncontrolled external use
Approve the provider contract, processing purposes, retention and location. Verify
restrictions on training/reuse and support access. Use minimum necessary content
and a local/manual fallback.
Required evidence: an intended-use statement, model/system inventory, hazard log and representative
validation set with lawful provenance. Agree acceptance thresholds before testing; report missed critical errors,
false matches, abstentions and reviewer corrections by workflow. A small synthetic corpus cannot support a
general accuracy claim.
GDPR Article 22 also needs assessment if a future workflow makes solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant
effects. The proposed design requires meaningful human judgment and authority to reject the recommendation, rather than a nominal
approval click. [R17]
Support staff AI literacy through training on limitations, source checking, escalation and incident reporting. Record training and
practical exercises. If classified high-risk, add the applicable risk/quality management, documentation, conformity, monitoring and
deployer duties, including a fundamental-rights assessment where required. [R07]
Sources: [R06] AI Act Article 6 · [R07] AI Act literacy · [R08] AI Act Annex III · [R17] GDPR automated decisions

10 / SECURE PRODUCT LIFECYCLE
Build security into every release
A secure deployment depends on maintained software, a response process and trustworthy
updates.
Before release
Maintain the threat model and security requirements alongside design decisions. Review security-sensitive
changes independently. Run meaningful authorization, isolation, parser, approval and handoff tests. Add
dependency, secret, container and static-analysis checks; keep the results with the release. Generate a
machine-readable software bill of materials, pin dependencies and verify build provenance.
Deliver and maintain safely
Sign release artifacts and verify them before deployment. Document secure defaults, upgrades, backup
compatibility, rollback constraints and supported versions. Create a vulnerability-reporting channel with a
responsible response team, severity triage and coordinated disclosure. Set patch targets based on exposure and
exploitability; emergency fixes must have a tested distribution path and customer communications.
If CRA applies
Evidence to prepare
Product scope and risk
Document the manufacturer role, intended use, component/remote-processing
boundary and product classification. Determine the required conformity
assessment rather than assuming self-assessment or third-party certification.
Technical documentation
Trace applicable essential requirements to design, test results and residual-risk
decisions. Record the support period and user-facing security instructions.
Vulnerability handling
Maintain intake, triage, remediation, notification and lifecycle records. Connect
actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents to the CRA
reporting procedure.
Market obligations
Complete the applicable conformity route and declaration/CE requirements when
required. Neither this report nor an ISO certificate establishes product conformity.
Release evidence should be reproducible
Each release should identify its source revision, dependencies, build artifacts, deployment configuration,
tests, approvals, known limits and support status. Keep confidential test data out of public repositories and
automated external services.

11 / INCIDENT RESPONSE
Prepare for different reporting
clocks
One event can trigger several regimes. Track awareness times, thresholds, recipients and
follow-up obligations separately.
Regime / trigger
Notification path and timing
GDPR
Personal-data breach
The processor informs the controller without undue delay. The controller notifies
the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72
hours of awareness, unless the breach is unlikely to risk individuals'
rights/freedoms. Inform affected people without undue delay where high risk
requires it. Record all breaches and decisions. [R04]
NIS2, if in scope
Significant incident
Generally: early warning within 24 hours; incident notification within 72 hours;
final report within one month of the incident notification. If ongoing, follow the
progress/final-report rules. Confirm national recipients, significance criteria and
special-sector exceptions. [R12]
CRA, if in scope
Actively exploited vulnerability
Manufacturer: early warning within 24 hours of awareness; vulnerability
notification within 72 hours. Final report within 14 days after a corrective or
mitigating measure becomes available. Use the required reporting platform and
recipients. [R11]
CRA, if in scope
Severe product-security incident
Manufacturer: early warning within 24 hours; incident notification within 72
hours; final report within one month after the incident notification. Assess
affected-user communications and required updates. [R11]
Operational response sequence
Detect and preserve: alert the incident lead, record awareness, secure relevant logs and preserve evidence
without copying entire case files unnecessarily. Contain: revoke affected credentials, isolate workers, suspend
risky exports and preserve a manual office workflow. Assess: involve the office, privacy/legal advisers and
suppliers; classify each reporting threshold. Recover: restore trusted versions, reconcile receipts and confirm
key integrity. Learn: assign corrective actions and retest.
Required before live use: named primary/deputy contacts, a maintained authority/CSIRT directory, preapproved report templates and
a tabletop exercise covering ransomware, wrong-recipient export and compromised AI/API credentials. Contractual supplier notice
targets must leave the controller enough time to act. Do not wait for a complete forensic report before a legally required initial
notification.

12 / RETENTION AND RESILIENCE
Recover safely. Delete deliberately.
Availability and lawful retention must be designed together, including the copies held outside
the live database.
Data class
Required policy and implementation
Originals, email and OCR
Specify the purpose, retention trigger, authorized holds and deletion evidence.
Existing cleanup begins after acknowledged delivery and removes source material;
verify orphaned files, temporary files and retry failures.
Cases and approvals
Define schedules for structured values, review revisions, frozen snapshots, exports
and receipts. The existing source purge does not erase these. Apply notarial record
duties and rights-request exceptions before deletion.
Logs and analytics
Minimize personal content, limit readers and set a justified duration. Separate
security evidence from operational debugging. Do not keep complete prompts or
case text as default diagnostics.
Backups and suppliers
Define expiry, restoration restrictions and deletion/return on exit. A restore must
not silently resurrect information due for deletion; replay approved deletion/hold
decisions. Verify supplier copies and support attachments.
Current boundary: the configured pilot source-retention value is not a legal retention rule. The office must
approve a schedule for each data class. Required legal holds need controlled authorization, scope, review and
release; unrestricted storage is not an acceptable substitute.
Recovery target and proof
Agree maximum tolerable data loss (RPO) and recovery time (RTO) through an office impact assessment. An
initial planning target could be RPO ≤ 4 hours and RTO ≤ 8 hours, subject to office approval and architecture
changes. These are proposed engineering targets, not current measurements or legal minima.
Protect off-site backups and the recovery key under separate custody. Test fresh-target restoration and the
compromise of the primary operator account. The current backup procedure requires draining all writers;
meeting the target may require coordinated snapshots or a validated continuous-recovery design. Reconcile
approved cases, vendor receipts and separately retained latest audit anchors after recovery.
Required evidence: timed restoration in the actual environment, documented writer consistency, integrity verification, independent
anchor comparison, successful key recovery, deletion/hold reconciliation and a demonstrated manual continuity procedure. Repeat
after major changes and on a risk-based schedule; quarterly drills are the proposed initial cadence.

13 / GOVERNANCE AND ASSURANCE
Give every control an owner
Strong assurance is a management practice supported by technical evidence and independent
review.
Accountability
Responsibility
Office leadership / notary
Approve intended use, professional-secrecy decisions, staff roles, retention and
residual operational risk. Accept the office-specific deployment and handoff.
Product owner
Control feature scope, legal classification triggers and public claims. Own the
release evidence pack and supplier interface commitments.
Security + platform lead
Own threat modelling, hardening, keys, monitoring, vulnerability management,
recovery and incident command. Maintain named deputies.
Privacy/legal advisers; DPO
where required
Advise on lawful basis, DPIA, professional duties, processor/transfer terms and
notifications. Preserve DPO independence; management remains accountable.
Engineering + AI quality owner
Implement preventive controls, regression and adversarial tests, representative
evaluation, change control and safety monitoring.
Independent assessor
Challenge the deployment and evidence, test security boundaries and verify
remediation. Keep the scope and limitations explicit.
Voluntary standards as an assurance framework
Use ISO/IEC 27001:2022 to organize information-security risk management and the control system. Consider
ISO/IEC 42001:2023 for governance of AI development and use. Establish a defined scope, management
reviews, internal audits and corrective actions; then consider independent certification when operating evidence
is mature. [R14, R15]
Keep claims precise
An ISO management-system certificate has a defined organization and scope. It does not certify every
product feature, satisfy every EU law or replace an applicable CE/conformity process. Do not claim
“EU-certified,” “fully GDPR compliant,” “zero risk” or “ProNotar integrated” without specific, defensible
evidence.
Proposed cadence: monthly risk and incident review; quarterly access, supplier and recovery review; independent assessment before
launch and after material boundary changes. Recheck relevant harmonised standards and their actual legal scope before relying on a
presumption of conformity.
